Status: Draft for adoption review. A named safeguarding lead, verified confidential reporting channel, referral map, safer-recruitment procedure and response timelines have not been confirmed.

1. Purpose, scope and rights-based approach

This draft applies to foundation leaders, staff, volunteers, mentors, contractors, partners and visitors involved in activities with children. A child is a person under 18. The child’s best interests are a primary consideration; every child must be treated with dignity, without discrimination, and have an age-appropriate opportunity to be heard.

The policy is guided by Article 53 of the Constitution of Kenya, the Children Act 2022, the UNCRC (including Articles 2, 3, 12, 16, 19 and 34), and the African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child (including Articles 3, 4, 10, 16, 21 and 27).

2. Minimum standards for adults

  • Put children’s safety and best interests before convenience, reputation or programme targets.
  • Use respectful, age-appropriate language; never humiliate, threaten, exploit, discriminate against or physically punish a child.
  • Avoid unnecessary one-to-one contact in private or hidden settings. Use observable, approved settings and appropriate adult supervision.
  • Do not develop secret, sexualised or dependent relationships, request intimate images, exchange private social-media accounts, or offer gifts or transport in ways that bypass guardians and programme safeguards.
  • Respect a child’s privacy. Do not share names, images, stories, school details or case information without a lawful purpose and separately documented authorisation; seek the child’s age-appropriate assent as well.
  • Do not promise a child secrecy about a safety concern. Explain that information may need to be shared with people who can help protect them.

3. Safer recruitment, supervision and activities

Before this policy is adopted, the foundation must approve and implement role descriptions, reference and suitability checks, any legally required vetting, safeguarding induction, supervision, incident recording and partner due diligence. This draft does not claim that those checks or training are currently in place.

Activities should have a named responsible adult, clear supervision arrangements, safe venues, emergency planning and accessible ways for children to raise concerns. A guardian’s contact details on an application do not by themselves prove identity, authority or informed consent.

Referrals may be made by teachers, credible community leaders or known well-wishers, with supporting information requested where needed. This referral route does not make the referrer a parent or legal guardian, and does not replace the required guardian-consent process.

The foundation’s stated procedure is to contact a parent or guardian before any programme action and obtain consent when another person makes the referral. The current web form does not enforce this sequence; it can receive and store learner details before that contact. The safeguarding and lawful basis for such an initial referral must be reviewed, and the form process updated before adoption.

4. Receiving and responding to a concern

  1. Listen calmly, take the child seriously and check whether anyone is in immediate danger.
  2. Do not investigate, confront the alleged person, ask leading questions or circulate the account.
  3. Record only necessary, factual information and protect it from unauthorised access.
  4. Escalate promptly to the designated safeguarding lead once the foundation appoints and publishes that contact. Where there is immediate danger or a legal reporting duty, contact the appropriate emergency or child-protection authority without waiting for an internal response.
  5. Take steps to prevent retaliation and keep the child and their guardian appropriately informed, subject to safety and law.

5. Child information, images and digital safety

Collect the minimum information needed, explain the purpose in child-friendly language, use an appropriate guardian-consent and age-verification process where required, and respect the child’s evolving capacity and views. Do not place identifiable child information in the website chatbot or public social-media messages.

Photos, testimonials and case stories require separate permission for the specific use, review for risk of identification or stigma, and the child’s appropriate assent. A programme application is not permission to publish a child’s image or story.

6. Accountability and review

The board or authorised leadership must appoint a safeguarding lead, approve reporting and referral steps, keep a restricted incident register, review concerns for learning, and communicate this policy to staff, volunteers and partners. The lead’s identity, training, response targets and review cycle are still to be confirmed.
