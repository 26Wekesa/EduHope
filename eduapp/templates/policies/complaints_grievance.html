Status: Draft. The foundation has set a target for an initial response within seven calendar days. An independent escalation contact, complaint owner and formal review/appeal arrangements have not been approved.

1. Who may raise a concern

Any learner, parent or guardian, donor, mentor, volunteer, staff member, partner or community member may raise a complaint about a service, decision, payment, privacy issue, discrimination, misconduct, safeguarding concern or suspected misuse of resources.

Children should be able to raise concerns in language and ways they can understand, with support from a trusted adult if they choose. Their safety, dignity, privacy and views must be respected.

2. How to contact the foundation

The current general routes are Contact Us and boldfoundation59@gmail.com or +254 793 299 818. Include only the information needed to explain the issue, how you would like to be contacted, and any immediate safety concern. Do not send a child’s sensitive case details through Facebook or other public social media.

The general inbox is not a verified confidential or independent complaints channel. The foundation Secretary monitors the general email, but the foundation must designate an independent escalation route for complaints involving senior leadership before adopting this policy. Anonymous concerns may be considered where possible, but may limit follow-up.

3. Fair handling principles

  • Listen respectfully and acknowledge the concern without prejudging its outcome.
  • Assess immediate risk first; safeguarding concerns must follow the Child Safeguarding Policy and must not wait for an ordinary complaint process.
  • Use an impartial person who is not implicated; preserve relevant records and share information only with those who need it.
  • Explain the next step and any limits on confidentiality, and provide reasonable accessibility or language support.
  • Protect people from retaliation for raising a concern in good faith.
  • Record the complaint, actions and outcome securely, and use themes to improve services without identifying complainants unnecessarily.

4. Acknowledgement, outcome and escalation

The foundation aims to send an initial acknowledgement or response within seven calendar days. This is not a promise that an investigation or remedy will be completed within that period. If more time is needed, the complainant should be told what happens next and when an update is expected. A formal review or appeal option and independent escalation contact must still be approved.

Where a complaint concerns personal-data handling, a person may also contact the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner. Where a child is in immediate danger, contact the appropriate emergency or child-protection authority rather than waiting for an internal response.
