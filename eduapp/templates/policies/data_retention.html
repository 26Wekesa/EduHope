Status: Draft schedule only. The site code does not establish or enforce category-specific deletion periods, and no approved retention schedule was supplied.

1. Retention rule

Personal information should be adequate, relevant and limited to the stated purpose, accurate, securely protected and kept in identifiable form no longer than necessary. The foundation must document the purpose, lawful basis, start/end event, retention period, review owner, deletion method and any lawful hold for each record category.

2. Draft retention schedule — periods require approval

Record category Examples Approved period Required owner/action
Child support and referral Learner identity, circumstances, guardian and informant details Not set Set a child-centred period and review/closure trigger with the safeguarding and programme leads.
Mentor, booking and partnership records Applications, contact details, notes and decisions Not set Define periods for unsuccessful applications, active relationships and closed requests.

Chat assistant content Question and recent turns sent to Google Gemini Provider terms not verified The application code does not save chat text to its own application database; verify Google and hosting logs/retention before enabling this for child-related queries.

3. Deletion, backup and legal holds

When a period expires, records should be securely deleted or irreversibly anonymised from active systems. Backup copies, exports and vendor-held copies must have documented expiry or deletion controls. A legal hold may pause deletion only for the records and period necessary, with restricted access and a recorded review date.

The current application contains no scheduled deletion job or user-facing account/data deletion workflow. This policy must not claim that automatic deletion is already operating. A responsible owner must implement and periodically test the approved schedule before adoption.

4. Children’s records

Children’s information requires heightened protection. Keep only the minimum case details needed, restrict access to authorised people, avoid unnecessary copies, and set a review point when support ends. Retention decisions must balance the child’s privacy and safety with any genuine legal, safeguarding, accounting or dispute obligation; indefinite retention is not a default.

5. Governance and review

The board or authorised leadership must approve the schedule, assign an owner for each category, record exceptions and legal holds, and review it at least annually and whenever a programme or provider changes. See the Privacy Policy.
