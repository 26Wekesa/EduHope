Status: Draft for review. The legal controller identity, privacy lead, lawful-basis mapping, international-transfer safeguards and retention periods require confirmation before this can be treated as final.

1. Who is responsible

This website uses the name Bold Foundation. The foundation’s full registered legal name, registration details and designated data-protection contact have not been verified for this draft. The site publishes boldfoundation59@gmail.com and +254 793 299 818 as general contact routes; neither is represented here as a formally appointed Data Protection Officer. The Foundation Secretary monitors the general email, but no formally appointed privacy officer has been identified.

2. Information the current website asks for

  • Learner-support applications: a learner’s name, date of birth, grade, school, requested support, circumstances, guardian name and contact details, address, and information about the person making the referral.
  • Mentor applications: name, email, phone, organisation, years of experience, subject and message; a linked account may be used for a mentor portal.
  • School bookings and partnerships: contact and organisation details, requested activities, proposed dates, participant numbers and free-text notes.
  • Contact requests: name, email, subject and message.
  • Event donations: payer name/contact details, phone number, amount, payment status and M-Pesa transaction references. The payment request is sent to Safaricom’s M-Pesa service. Mentor applications are free and do not involve payment information.
  • Accounts and security: username, email, password hash, session and anti-forgery tokens, and information needed to protect the service.
  • Chat assistant: the question and recent conversation turns are sent to Google’s Gemini API when the assistant is used. Do not enter a child’s name, contact details, case history, health information or other confidential information into the chatbot.
  • Technical security: the chatbot rate limit temporarily uses a visitor IP address in a cache key. Hosting and security providers may also process request metadata; exact log fields and retention require confirmation.
  • Event content: authorised administrators can upload event photos and reports. These may contain identifiable people and must not include a child without the required permissions and safeguarding review.

Free-text descriptions can reveal sensitive information. Only submit information that is necessary and that you are authorised to provide. Children and guardians should receive an age-appropriate explanation before any child-related information is collected.

3. Why information is used and lawful basis

The site uses information to respond to enquiries, assess and coordinate programme requests, manage accounts, arrange event payments, maintain security, and meet legal duties. The foundation must document the lawful basis for each purpose under the Data Protection Act, and must not treat a general terms checkbox as a substitute for a lawful basis or valid consent.

For a child’s personal data, processing must meet the applicable child-specific legal safeguards, be in the child’s best interests, and use an appropriate parent or guardian consent and age-verification process where required. The current learner form collects guardian details but this review did not identify a distinct verified guardian-consent step. That gap must be resolved before this policy is adopted.

Referrals may come from teachers, credible community leaders or known well-wishers, and supporting information may be requested. A referrer’s role or reputation does not itself establish parent or guardian authority or consent.

The foundation’s stated procedure is to contact the parent or guardian before taking programme action and to obtain their consent when a third party makes a referral. The current website form does not enforce that sequence and stores the submitted child details before a guardian-consent check. The lawfulness and minimum-data approach for that initial referral must be confirmed, and the workflow must be changed before this draft can be treated as final.

The current Terms acceptance marker is stored in a server session. It is not a durable record of acceptance date/version linked to an applicant, and it is not a separate privacy-consent record. The registration flow also needs review before the foundation can claim it records informed policy acceptance.

4. Sharing and processing outside Kenya

Information may be accessible to authorised foundation administrators and to providers needed to run the service, including Render for hosting/database infrastructure, Safaricom for M-Pesa payment processing, and Google when the Gemini chatbot is used. The website also links to external social networks; those services receive information when a visitor follows a link.

The foundation’s stated supporter reach is international, while its beneficiary programmes focus on children in Kenya. If personal information is collected from people in other countries, the foundation must assess any additional privacy laws that apply; this draft does not claim global compliance.

The checked-in Render configuration specifies a PostgreSQL database region in Oregon, United States. The actual production location and provider terms must be verified. Before cross-border transfers are made, the foundation must document the destination, processor arrangements and safeguards required by Kenyan law. No claim is made here that those transfer checks are complete.

Personal information is not intentionally collected for advertising, and no analytics or marketing tracker is currently present in the site templates reviewed.

5. Children’s dignity, rights and privacy

The foundation’s work must respect each child’s dignity, equality, privacy, safety, participation and best interests. Children should receive explanations they can understand, have their views heard in an age-appropriate way, and not be exposed through public stories or images without separate, informed authorisation and the child’s appropriate assent.

The privacy commitments should be read with the Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. A parent or guardian’s contact details are not proof that consent was obtained or verified.

6. Storage, security and retention

Access should be limited to people who need the information for an authorised purpose. The foundation must verify its actual access controls, backups, encryption, breach-response process and vendor safeguards; this draft does not promise encryption at rest or a particular security certification.

No approved category-by-category retention schedule or automatic deletion process was identified. Exact periods must be set and implemented before adoption. See the Data Retention Policy draft.

7. Your rights and how to make a request

Subject to applicable law, a person may ask to be informed about processing, access their personal data, correct inaccurate data, object to processing, request erasure where available, and withdraw consent where consent is the basis. A guardian may act for a child where legally authorised, while respecting the child’s best interests and evolving capacity.

Send a request to boldfoundation59@gmail.com, call +254 793 299 818, or use Contact Us. The foundation must confirm the identity and authority needed for a request without collecting excessive additional data. If a concern is not resolved, a complaint may be raised with the ODPC. The foundation's target is an initial response within seven calendar days; this is not a promise that a complex request will be completed within that time. A privacy-response owner still needs to be designated.

8. Related policies and changes

Read the Cookie Policy, Complaints and Grievance Policy, and Donations and Refund Policy. Any revised version should show its effective date and version, and material changes should be communicated in an accessible way.
